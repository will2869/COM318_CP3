Space debris is not science fiction anymore.

Protect orbit before debris blocks future missions.

Satellites help power weather forecasting, communication, mapping, research, and emergency response. The goal of this campaign is not to stop space innovation. The goal is to keep space usable by pairing satellite growth with real funding for debris cleanup.

Campaign Goal

Persuade young adults, especially students and early-career technology professionals, to support dedicated funding for orbital debris removal research and demonstration missions.

The issue

Why orbital debris matters

Debris moves extremely fast

Even small objects can cause serious damage because debris in orbit travels at very high speed.

More launches mean more crowding

Satellite constellations can improve life on Earth, but crowded orbits increase the need for better rules and cleanup funding.

Cleanup needs stable support

Voluntary guidelines help reduce future debris, but active cleanup requires funding, testing, and long-term accountability.

The solution

Satellite growth and cleanup funding can happen together.

Space technology should continue to expand, but expansion should include enforceable funding for cleanup research, demonstration missions, and responsible end-of-life disposal. This protects the orbital environment without framing satellite progress as the enemy.

  • Fund active debris removal demonstrations.
  • Support stronger post-mission disposal standards.
  • Create incentives for companies that design safer missions.
  • Make debris cleanup part of long-term space infrastructure planning.

Common questions

Quick answers

Is this campaign anti-satellite?

No. The campaign supports satellite growth, but argues that growth should come with funding and accountability for cleanup.

Why should young adults care?

Space debris threatens services people use every day, including GPS, weather data, communication, internet infrastructure, and research.

Can we actually remove debris?

Several technologies are being researched and tested, but the issue is funding and scale. Cleanup cannot become routine without stable support.
